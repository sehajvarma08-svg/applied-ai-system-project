# Listening contexts

Each `##` section is one retrievable chunk. Keep sections short and self-contained.

## Studying and deep work
Focus music should be low in energy (about 0.3-0.5), mostly instrumental or lyric-light, and steady in tempo (70-95 BPM). Lofi and ambient fit best; the moods "focused" and "chill" are the closest catalog labels. Avoid high-valence, high-energy pop, since lyrics and big hooks pull attention away.

## Working out and running
Workout music wants high energy (0.8+), a fast tempo (125-160 BPM) and strong danceability. Moods "intense" and "euphoric" fit. Genres: rock, edm, pop, hip hop, metal. Acoustic songs (acousticness above 0.6) rarely work here.

## Falling asleep and winding down
Pick very low energy (below 0.3), slow tempo (under 80 BPM) and high acousticness (0.7+). Moods "serene", "relaxed" and "chill". Genres: classical, ambient, folk, lofi. Avoid anything "intense" or "angry".

## Late-night driving
Night drives suit mid energy (0.5-0.7), a steady 100-120 BPM pulse and a "moody" or "dark" feel. Synthwave and electronic are the natural genres; hip hop also works.

## Parties and gatherings
High danceability (0.75+), high valence (0.7+) and high energy. Moods "festive", "euphoric", "happy". Genres: latin, edm, pop, reggae, hip hop.

## Rainy days and reflection
Low-to-mid energy, lower valence (under 0.5) and high acousticness. Moods "melancholic", "nostalgic", "moody". Genres: folk, country, jazz, indie pop.

## Cooking dinner or a date night
Warm, medium-low energy (0.4-0.6) with smooth rhythm. Moods "romantic", "relaxed", "laid-back". Genres: r&b, jazz, latin, reggae.

## Morning and commuting
Uplifting mid-high energy (0.6-0.8) with high valence (0.7+). Moods "happy" and "confident". Genres: pop, indie pop, hip hop.
