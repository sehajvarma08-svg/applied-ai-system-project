# Mood glossary

The catalog uses these exact mood labels: angry, chill, confident, dark, euphoric, festive, focused, happy, intense, laid-back, melancholic, moody, nostalgic, relaxed, romantic, serene.

## Near-synonyms and how to map them
The scorer matches mood strings exactly, so a user's wording must be mapped to a catalog label. "Calm", "peaceful", "mellow" map to chill, relaxed or serene. "Sad", "blue", "heartbroken" map to melancholic. "Pumped", "hyped", "aggressive" map to intense (or angry for rage). "Cheerful", "upbeat", "joyful" map to happy. "Sentimental", "throwback" map to nostalgic. "Sexy", "love" map to romantic. "Spooky", "gloomy", "brooding" map to dark or moody. "Productive", "concentrating" map to focused.

## Chill vs relaxed vs serene
Chill is low-energy but still rhythmic (lofi beats). Relaxed is warm and unhurried (jazz). Serene is the quietest and most acoustic (classical, choral). If a user wants to sleep, prefer serene; to work, prefer chill or focused.

## Intense vs angry vs dark
Intense is high-energy and driving (gym, rock). Angry is intense with aggression and low valence (metal). Dark is lower energy but heavy and low valence (electronic, synthwave).

## Happy vs euphoric vs festive
Happy is bright and moderate-to-high energy. Euphoric is the peak: very high energy and danceability (edm). Festive is communal and rhythmic (latin).

## Melancholic vs nostalgic vs moody
Melancholic is sad and acoustic (folk). Nostalgic is bittersweet and warm (country). Moody is ambiguous, nocturnal and mid-energy (synthwave).
